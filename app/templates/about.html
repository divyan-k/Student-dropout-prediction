System Architecture & Methodology

← Return to Risk Prediction Workspace
System Overview: Many dropout models depend on data that only becomes available late. This system provides three predictions as information accumulates: at admission, after semester 1, and after semester 2. Stage 1 is a coarse screening signal; accuracy and reliability improve markedly once semester results are available. All results below are from a held-out test set (20% of the data, n = 885, never used in tuning) unless labeled CV; cross-validation figures are in the full report. The model was trained on one Portuguese institution’s data (Polytechnic Institute of Portalegre) and predictions are relative risk bands, not calibrated probabilities.

Staged Prediction Paradigm

To accommodate the natural chronological availability of academic data in university administration, models are partitioned into three life-cycle stages:

Stage Milestone Name Input Scope Evaluation Moment
Stage 1 Admission Profile (24 features) Demographics, socio-economic background, parental education/occupations, high school qualification, fee status at data collection Before first semester classes begin
Stage 2 Post-Semester 1 (30 features) Stage 1 features + 1st semester enrolled, approved, evaluations, grades, and pass rate Following 1st semester exam results
Stage 3 Post-Semester 2 (36 features) Stage 2 features + 2nd semester curricular outcomes, cumulative credits, and grade momentum End of first academic year
Stage 1 Diagnostic Guidance: Stage 1 is an early screening signal suitable for flagging cohorts for light monitoring, not for judging an individual student. At admission, test accuracy is 61.0% (against 49.9% for majority guessing) and dropout recall is 62.0% (up from 56.0%), successfully identifying over 60% of future dropouts pre-enrollment. Its multi-class ROC-AUC (OvR) of 0.768 confirms solid ranking ability across student cohorts.

Model Performance & Benchmarks (Option A: Random Forest Architecture)

To prioritize the early-warning mission of the system, Random Forest was adopted across all three prediction stages. All candidate models were evaluated using paired 5-fold Stratified Cross-Validation on the training partition (n = 3,539). Multi-class balanced sample weights were applied uniformly. All performance scores below are evaluated on the held-out test partition (n = 885, evaluated strictly once), with cross-validation training stability noted.

Subgroup Demographic & Financial Fairness Audit

Critical Finding & Advisory Warning: A low-risk prediction does not mean a student is safe. While the model catches 95.7% of dropouts with overdue tuition fees, its recall drops to 51.3% (Stage 2) and 56.5% (Stage 3) for students whose tuition fees are current—a massive 39 to 44 percentage point gap. Students with clean financial records represent 67% of all dropouts in the test set (191 of 284). Financial distress creates an overwhelming statistical signal, which can mask academic attrition among financially secure students. Advisors must never assume financially current students are safe.

Audit Notes on Small Subgroups: Scholarship holders have only 19 dropouts in the test set (recall 57.9%, 95% CI [36.3%, 76.9%]); International students have only 9 dropouts (95% CI [26.7%, 81.1%]); and Special Needs students have only 4 dropouts (95% CI [15.0%, 85.0%]). These wide margins of error confirm that observed recall variations in small groups reflect sample variance rather than systematic algorithmic bias.

Ethical AI, Limitations & Provenance

  • Decision-Support Only: Predictions are statistical early warnings designed solely for academic mentors and counselors. They must never be used to automate punitive administrative actions, deny enrollment, or revoke financial support.
  • Uncalibrated Probabilities: Model probabilities are uncalibrated ensemble outputs; risk levels represent relative risk rankings rather than absolute frequentist probabilities.
  • Single Institution Provenance: The underlying dataset originates from a single Portuguese higher education institution (Polytechnic Institute of Portalegre). Curricular dynamics, grading scales, and tuition policies differ across institutions, limiting direct generalizability without local re-calibration.
  • Temporal Snapshot Nuance: Socio-economic indicators (such as debtor status and tuition payment status) represent point-in-time snapshots at data collection and may fluctuate dynamically over a student's degree lifecycle.
  • Transitional Ambiguity of Enrolled Students: Intermediate "Enrolled" students represent students still actively progressing or delayed. Because they share characteristics with both graduates and dropouts, multi-class Macro-F1 reflects this inherent label ambiguity.
  • Human-Interpretable SHAP: Explanations aggregate signed SHAP attributions across one-hot columns back to original domain concepts (e.g. "Semester 1 pass rate" rather than internal column codes) to ensure full transparency for academic advisors.
